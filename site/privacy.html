Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 2, 2026

This policy explains what data the Suit Up! app ("the app") uses, where that data goes, and the choices you have.

1. Data that stays on your device

The following data is stored only on your device. It is not sent to the developer or to any server:

  • Your suits (name, icon, and photos you add).
  • Your sessions (suit, event, and venue).
  • Events and venues you create, including their coordinates.
  • Your settings and background image.

Photos you pick from your library or take with the camera are resized and saved inside the app. They are never uploaded.

2. Location

If you allow it, the app uses your approximate location while the app is in use. It compares your position to known cons, meets, and venues to tag your session automatically. The app does not use location in the background.

Your coordinates are not sent to the developer or to the analytics provider. To show a place name (for example a city or street), the app asks your phone's built-in geocoding service, which is provided by Apple on iOS and Google on Android. Their privacy policies apply to that request.

You can refuse or remove location access at any time in your device settings. The app still works, and you can pick events manually.

3. Maps

The app shows maps from Apple Maps (iOS) or Google Maps (Android). When a map is shown, the map provider loads map tiles for the visible area and may receive standard technical data such as your IP address. See the Apple Privacy Policy and the Google Privacy Policy.

4. Anonymous usage data (optional)

If Share anonymous usage data is on (Settings -> About), the app sends anonymous analytics to PostHog, hosted in the European Union. This helps fix bugs and decide what to improve. It is on by default, and you can turn it off at any time. When it is off, the app sends no analytics.

When it is on, the app sends:

  • Feature usage: events such as "session started", "suit created", or "Year in Review opened", with simple details such as durations, counts, and the number of breaks.
  • Aggregate counts: at most once a day, totals such as the number of suits and sessions, total suited minutes, and active days. These power the Year in Review comparisons.
  • App lifecycle: when the app is installed, updated, opened, or closed.
  • Crash and error reports: error messages and technical details to help fix problems.
  • Technical data: app version, operating system and version, language, and a random ID created when the app is installed.
  • Feedback: if you use Send feedback, the text you write.

The app does not send your name, your precise location or coordinates, your photos, the names of your suits, or your device name or model. The app asks PostHog not to use your IP address to determine your location. The random ID is not linked to your identity, and the data is not used to track you across other apps or websites.

5. Sharing

When you share a Year in Review card, the app creates an image on your device and opens your system share sheet. The app sends it only where you choose.

The developer does not sell your data and does not share it with advertisers. The app contains no ads.

6. Data retention and deletion

  • On-device data: kept until you delete it in the app or delete the app.
  • Analytics data: kept by PostHog for a limited period, then deleted. Because this data is anonymous, it cannot normally be linked to you. You can still ask for deletion by email (see below).

7. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or CCPA), you may have the right to access, correct, or delete data about you, or to object to processing. Analytics are based on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time with the in-app switch. To make a request, contact the developer.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
